# Analyze a Product Flow

Analyze one bounded journey without turning every surface into a page-level deep dive.

## Define the journey

Establish:

- actor, goal, entry condition, and successful outcome;
- first and last included surfaces;
- prerequisites and relevant product state;
- alternative, abandon, error, recovery, and resume paths;
- excluded upstream and downstream behavior.

## Map observable steps

For each step, record:

- surface and user intent;
- primary action and feedback;
- information needed for the decision;
- navigation or transition;
- branching, validation, permission, or lifecycle gate;
- evidence label and confidence.

Do not infer hidden backend behavior or business rules from the visible sequence.

## Evaluate the flow

Assess applicable:

- information architecture and orientation;
- continuity and context preservation;
- decision points and cognitive load;
- friction, uncertainty, and dead ends;
- trust and consequence communication;
- error and recovery behavior;
- desktop, mobile, locale, auth, or plan variation;
- accessibility signals that can actually be observed;
- product-specific strengths and weaknesses.

Do not claim conversion or user satisfaction without supporting evidence.

## Select deep-dive candidates

Identify only surfaces where a deeper analysis could change the target decision. Explain why each candidate matters and which focused dimension is unresolved.

Return the flow result before continuing into unspecified deep dives.
