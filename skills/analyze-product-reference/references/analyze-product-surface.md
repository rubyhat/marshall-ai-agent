# Analyze a Product Surface

Analyze one coherent user-facing surface at product and interaction level.

## Establish context

Record:

- actor, goal, route or entry, and place in the journey;
- observed device, viewport, locale, authentication, plan, and data state;
- source date, evidence scope, and missing variants;
- the surface's apparent responsibility.

## Inspect the surface

Cover only applicable dimensions:

- information and visual hierarchy;
- content sequence and progressive disclosure;
- primary, secondary, passive, and destructive actions;
- navigation, entry, exit, back, cancel, and continuation;
- ready, loading, empty, pending, validation, error, success, and permission states;
- forms, selections, filters, search, comparison, or data density;
- trust, pricing, consequence, legal, privacy, or support communication;
- responsive behavior and observable accessibility cues;
- consistency with the reference product's surrounding experience.

Separate what is visible from why the product may have chosen it.

## Evaluate without copying

Describe:

- what works and for which apparent user need;
- what creates friction, ambiguity, risk, or mismatch;
- which patterns are structural versus merely visual;
- which behavior is specific to the reference product's business model;
- which variants remain unknown.

Do not produce pixel measurements, exact copy, component names, or technical architecture unless the user explicitly requested that exact evidence and it is lawful and useful.

## Produce implications

Return principles and target-product questions. Leave the target interaction contract to the design workflow and implementation detail to the specification workflow.
